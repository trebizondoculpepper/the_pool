# The alarm printer becomes a Gateway 3000 MightyPrint 80

The owner liked the old teleprinter's paper but not the printer ("looks trash"), and asked for it re-modelled as an Epson FX-80, a Gateway 3000 MightyPrint 80 in our world, in dev68. The FX-80's 1983 user's manual is on bitsavers with a text layer, so every figure the model uses (420 × 347 × 100 mm, 160 characters a second, logic seeking, the 2K buffer, the panel's three buttons and four lamps, FF and LF working only off line, the knob on the right, the switch on the left, the paper path over the smoked separator) comes from it, with page numbers, in `doc/research/mightyprint-80.md`. Its colours could not be checked against a photograph this session and are recorded as remembered.

The machine is pure logic (`dot-matrix-printer.ts`): a buffer, a head that seeks the nearer end of each line and crosses it at 160 cps, a paper position in lines, and lamps. The paper effect was kept but its face turned into a ring of paper two forms long, each line drawn once as it prints and the texture scrolled by the paper's position, so the newest line is at the platen and older ones climb out of the lid upright to someone in front, as on the real machine. Following the manual meant the printed paper now goes over the back to a pile behind the stand; dev0's board, with a wall behind, keeps it falling forward over the lid. Gateway became a brand in `brands.ts`, its mark the PC's cow-spotted carton, moved into `core` so both draw the same drawing.

Headless screenshots run frames slowly (the rooms clamp a frame's delta at 0.1 s), so nothing printed in the pictures; the paper-printed pictures were taken with forty lines sent and stepped once at build, a temporary edit that was taken out again.
