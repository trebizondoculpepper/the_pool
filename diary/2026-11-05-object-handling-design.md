# 2026-11-05 · Object handling design for missions 209–211

The owner and I answered the questions Ahnaa attached to the three briefs, one at a time. Mission 209 now makes every stationary `bin-like` object a guaranteed target for USE or LET GO while holding an item, within its ordinary use range. Each bin has a circular local Y-height/radius target zone; the game computes a plausible underarm arc through it, then the object may ricochet inside with about 20–30% momentum loss and stays retrievable. All holdables are accepted and bins never fill. The engineer's office carton is fixed, box-like and intended to be bin-like. Fit restrictions, ceiling clearance and despawning wait.

Mission 210 covers `holdable` pick-up and ordinary USE placement on a supportable top: a direct, silent, eased move of about half a second, with the placement target fixed at the start. It does not route around obstacles. USE requires a clear aimed spot and all four bottom corners on the top; generic floor, wall, shelf, stack, socket and plug placement are outside this mission. Mission 211 gives LET GO its own Q/gamepad B/touch input, moves gamepad crouch to right-stick click, preserves USE while carrying, and sends non-bin releases on a fixed leisurely ballistic throw. Solid vertical bounds take roughly 20–30% momentum per rebound, supportable tops catch descending items, and a neutral sound plays on landing. Cross-room transfer, item-location persistence, water effects, material responses and mouse-only LET GO are later work.

The three mission plans give the shared-system implementation sequence and verification points. Existing room-specific carrying and carton code remains in place until an implementation agent replaces it through the shared controls and traits systems. Mission 211's solid-bound rebounds wait on 208's unfinished solid-bounds stage; its inputs and pure maths can start sooner.
