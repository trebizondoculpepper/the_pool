# 2026-11-03: The temperature controller's real valve and OFF detent

Mission 148 started after a project-agent broadcast. In both plant schematics the pool temperature controller had commanded a duplicate valve machine while the heating pipe carried a separate regulating-valve fitting. The duplicate node and link were removed; the existing in-line fitting now records `controlledBy` and the simulation graph emits that command relation from the same expanded model. PL2's fitting was corrected from a solenoid operator to a motor operator.

The world samples bulk pool temperature, so the Pt100 is now consistently described as a representative pool-return sensor before the exchanger. Its process sample links to the exchanger pool inlet, and the visual probe moved to the inlet pipe. The controller and asset-register labels no longer repeat “pool”. The new control-bus route exposed an existing binding edge case: a short pool node name won over the longer controller name, and the Olympic node catalogue omitted the plumbing schematic's nodes. Matching the longest name and including those nodes made both instrument conduit routes bind to their model links.

The controller's selector was correctly reachable by a front-facing ray, but its room binding ignored OFF. From AUTO the first turn selected OFF, reread AUTO from the world and snapped back, preventing a second turn to HAND. World version 77 gives OFF a saved position with zero demand, and the room binding now dispatches all three positions. The BAND dial had been view-local while the world used the old 0.1 K thermostat band; it is now saved at 1–10 K, defaults to 2 K, and drives the actual proportional demand. The same ray test covers the set-point and band dials. The owner asked Nicole to quantise the commanded valve opening to 0.1 steps, matching the controller's ten-segment bar. Nicole adjusted PL2-TC1's south-wall orientation and place at the owner's chosen spot, and the owner confirmed the wall item may overhang the stair foot's floor clearance.
