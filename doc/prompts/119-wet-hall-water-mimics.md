# 119: Wet hall fresh-water and dirty-water mimics

Status: research and plan drafted 2026-11-01; awaiting peer feedback. No implementation yet.

## Owner's request and decisions

Replace the combined WH-MP1 with a fresh-water mimic that answers **“Where is my fresh water going?”** Add a mirror-image dirty-water mimic that answers **“Where is the dirty water coming from?”** Both show Lounge, Olympic, Jacuzzi and Footbath branches with their current flow in m³/h. The owner chose eight modeled branch flowmeters, one clean and one dirty for each pool, so each displayed rate belongs to an instrument in the plant model. The fresh-water mimic moves to the inside face of the clean bay's west partition, at the spot viewed from `?room=wet_hall&x=-2.17&y=0.06&z=-5.61&yaw=272.7&pitch=2.0&zoom=1.267`; its proposed centre is approximately x = −4.8, z = −5.7, facing east. Verify the exact mounting height, sightline, working reach and clashes in the room before fixing its placement. Put the dirty-water mimic on the pit side, where its branches and pit can be read together; the final position is a layout decision.

The dirty board must say explicitly that **Jacuzzi and Footbath drain-downs go to the pit**, with the pit's later pumped discharge to sewer shown as a separate downstream path. Lounge and Olympic _pool_ drain-downs go directly to sewer and must not be counted as pit inflow; their filter or balance-tank waste may still reach the pit. Each dirty branch displays its total instantaneous pit inflow and indicates the actual active source, such as BACKWASH, RINSE, FILTER DRAIN or TANK DRAIN. The fresh board keeps the break-tank level and the booster's relevant status and controls; the dirty board keeps pit level, high alarm and discharge-pump status. Keep local-only reset and selectors local under the project rule.

## Research findings

- `src/game/plant/wet-hall-schematic.ts` has one combined mimic node, one make-up connection to a generic pool-plant-rooms node and one backwash connection from it. WH-G1 and WH-FT1 are both upstream of the break tank, on town-main intake. The outfall monitor is downstream of the pit. None measures an individual pool branch. `src/toolkit/gallery/logic/corridor-services.ts` tees the four make-up feeds off a ring, and joins Lounge and Olympic backwash before its wet-hall inlet: individual meters belong before those joins, not on the shared wet-hall pipes.
- `src/game/plant/pools.ts` computes actual `madeUp`, `backwashed` and `drained` volumes for each pool on each world step. It currently returns per-pool `madeUp` but accumulates pit-bound waste in one `backwashed` total. Its `INTO_THE_PIT` set contains `pool_03` and `pool_04` only; their drain-downs join their filter washes in the pit. The other two pools' drain-downs are counted separately for direct sewer discharge.
- `src/game/plant/wet-hall.ts` stores combined delivered make-up (`flow`) and combined pit inflow (`pitInflow`) as m³/h; neither contains a per-pool breakdown. `meter` records town-main inflow, which can differ from delivered make-up while the break tank fills or empties.
- The Jacuzzi and Footbath _simulation_ sends their drain-down and filter-wash volumes to the pit, but `src/game/plant/jacuzzi-schematic.ts` and `footbath-schematic.ts` currently end those lines at a gully and `corridor-services.ts` draws no spa-to-pit waste links. This model/drawing discrepancy must be resolved before presenting physical spa branch meters as connected to the pit. The owner's confirmed route for these drains is the pit.
- Lounge's modeled `toWaste` includes filter dump, rinse, filter drain-down and balance-tank drain as well as backwash (`lounge-valves.ts`). The water-balance step counts all of this as pit-bound `backwashed`; source indications must not miscall it all backwash.
- `water-balance.ts` currently accumulates Olympic and Jacuzzi tank-drain volumes in `spilt`, which the world delivers to neither pit nor sewer. Their physical destinations and water accounting must be resolved before a meter reports them; the proposed route for a tank's maintenance drain is through its filter-waste system to the pit, subject to review.
- For any pool still using the water model's once-a-day lumped backwash, a raw per-step volume divided by `dt` would create an implausible one-frame meter spike. Identify these pools and give them a time-resolved wash path before claiming a physical instantaneous meter. Model short transmitter damping and compare rounded displayed rates to avoid redraws every frame.
- `src/toolkit/gallery/logic/wet-hall-mimic.ts` draws the current combined, linear board. `src/toolkit/gallery/logic/room-equipment.ts` places it on the clean bay's north wall. The west partition is at x = −4.8 and runs across the requested view's z coordinate (`src/toolkit/gallery/logic/shell-rooms.ts`). The old control room has separate pool mimics; check whether these wet-hall boards also need copies there before implementation.
- The existing pool circulation flowmeters measure recirculation and are not valid sources for make-up or pit-branch figures. The existing pit floats and level probe can continue to supply pit status; the intake and outfall meters remain useful context, but cannot be apportioned into four measured branch rates.

## Acceptance

1. The appropriate enhanced networks contain four make-up branch meters and four pit-inflow branch meters upstream of any joins, with stable, derived tags and real connections; the physical pipes and instruments are drawn only from those networks. Reconcile the spa drain and wash schematics, cross-room links and corridor pipes with their simulated pit route. The two boards are separate tagged Penfold Controls nodes, not two views of one combined node. Their readouts bind to their corresponding sensor states.
2. The fresh board at the owner's chosen west-partition location shows break tank → booster → four pool branches, with each branch's actual delivered rate in m³/h and an aggregate that reconciles to the sum. It replaces WH-MP1; retain its tag for the replacement if the catalogue permits, and give the dirty board a new stable tag.
3. The dirty board mirrors that visual grammar: four pool branches → pit → pump/outfall, with each branch's actual pit-bound rate and an aggregate that reconciles to `pitInflow`. Source indicators distinguish backwash/rinse/dump and the various drain routes when relevant; Jacuzzi and Footbath show DRAIN when draining to the pit. The large pools' direct-to-sewer _pool_ drain-downs never appear as pit inflow.
4. Meters report zero when a branch is not flowing; branch readings change with make-up calls, booster isolation/trip, backwash/rinse, spa filter wash and spa drain-down. Existing mains intake and outfall totals remain distinct from branch figures.
5. Place the boards so they are legible and reachable without blocking the clean-bay door, meters, tank servicing, pipework, west ramp or pit access. Try each branded branch transmitter in a development room and the two Penfold Controls boards in dev39 before installation; each board bears its maker mark. Install copies of both boards in the old control room and equivalent views on the bridge (the owner, 2026-11-01), sharing readings and permitted remote actions with the local boards, while resets and selectors stay local. Inspect both in the live wet hall. Run the component benchmark for each new display.
6. Sensor tags and labels derive from the model; do not type them into the scene. Show a meter's normal ON/OFF status, and show FAULT only if a failure mode is modeled. If damped readings are persisted, update the world version and migrate existing saves, coordinating with mission 118's reserved version 67. Update the installed asset catalogue, tests, room bindings, documentation, TODO and Unreleased changelog. Include a long-run conservation test that checks the four branch rates against wet-hall totals on every step. Run the five project checks before commit.
