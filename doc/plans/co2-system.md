# The CO₂ system: supply, distribution, dosing and detection

The owner, 2026-10-31: CO₂ is dosed through a valve controller, not a pump (`doc/prompts/114-aqualis-dosing-controller.md`); the cylinders and the master valve go in the loading bay; the gas is carried through the building to a CO₂ port in the wall of each plant room that doses it, the route between not modelled pipe by pipe; in each such plant room a CO₂ valve controller doses it into the circulation, driven by the room's Dr Aqualis Trio. This note records the pure logic built for it, what it follows in the real world, its sizes, how it will be drawn later, and what is left for the owner. It builds on mission 109 (`doc/prompts/109-co2-supply.md`), whose stage 1 already made the cylinder supply's logic and its drawing.

Nothing here touches the world state, `world.ts`, the Trio's world integration, the schematics or any visuals. The seam for the world is `co2-plant.ts`: a `Co2Plant` state the world can hold, and `stepCo2Plant`, which takes each room's Trio demand and whether its water is moving and returns the grams of CO₂ for each pool's `Additions.co2`.

## The master valve and the building line (`co2-distribution.ts`)

- **The master valve**: a normally closed solenoid valve just inside the loading bay's wall, after the emergency isolation valve (mission 109's design; the gas-industry master shut-off solenoids a detection panel drives). Open only while switched on at its panel, powered (24 V) and not tripped. The detection's high alarm trips it, and it is reset at its panel in the loading bay (a local control), not while a detector still holds it.
- **The line**: one volume of gas, 12 litres (about 80 m of 15 mm copper, 13.6 mm bore), at one pressure, by the ideal gas law (about 88 g above atmospheric at 4 bar). Each step the regulator gives what is drawn and tops the line up to its outlet (4 bar, or less on a bank nearly spent), up to 10 kg/h, while the master valve passes. With the supply stopped, the dosing valves still drawing bring it down in a minute or two to their inlet pressure switches, where they stop and it holds; a leak empties it. A line nobody draws on holds its pressure (the regulator locks up).
- **The wall ports**: one for the Lounge (`plant_pool_01`) and one for the Olympic (`plant_pool_02`), each with its isolating ball valve; open, the room sees the line's pressure; shut, nothing (the gas trapped behind it is neglected).
- **Leaks**: a leaking joint on the room side of a port (g/h at 4 bar, scaled by the absolute pressure) gives its gas into that room's air; a leaking pigtail at the manifold gives the cylinders' gas into the loading bay, master valve or no.
- **Alarms**: BANK EMPTY, SUPPLY EXHAUSTED, MASTER VALVE TRIPPED, and LINE PRESSURE LOW (below 2.5 bar with the master valve switched on).

## The CO₂ valve controller (`co2-dosing.ts`)

A packaged pool CO₂ dosing panel on the wall beside the room's wall port, after the commercial CO₂ feed systems (Blue-White's, with its flow meter and stainless solenoid valve; Neptune Benson's and Chemtrol's, a normally closed solenoid, a rate-of-flow meter, and an injection nozzle or diffuser with its check valve) and the European gas dosing panels of Lutz-Jesco and ProMinent. Each has an inlet pressure switch, its own second-stage regulator (2 bar working, needing 0.3 bar above it), a dosing valve, a flow meter, a non-return valve, and the gas line to the injection point, whose back pressure is the booster line's water (1 bar). Two kinds, a bit of everything:

- **'solenoid'**, the Lounge's, the period's panel: a normally closed solenoid valve, a glass-tube rotameter with a needle valve setting the flow while it is open (300 g/h), and a limit contact on the float that proves the flow. The Trio's 4–20 mA is turned into time-proportional opening on a 60 s cycle, so the average is demand × the needle valve's setting.
- **'mass-flow'**, the Olympic's, the modern panel: a thermal mass flow controller, its proportional valve and meter in one body (after Bronkhorst's EL-FLOW and Brooks's SLA series, which take a 4–20 mA set point and give back the measured flow), range 2 kg/h, holding demand × its range whatever the inlet pressure, while it can.

Both take, from the Trio's CO₂ port, its 4–20 mA (4 mA nothing, 20 its most; below 3.6 mA a broken loop, NAMUR NE 43) and its release contact (closed while it asks for anything), as the brief's ports are; and give back its mode, power, fault relay and metered flow in g/h, which is `ChannelFeedback` exactly (`co2Feedback`; null with the control cable not connected). `trioSignal(demand, connected)` is the cable the other way.

- **Its selector**, REMOTE · MANUAL · OFF, a local control on the panel, as `ChannelFeedback.mode`: REMOTE doses as the Trio asks while its release is closed; MANUAL at its own rate (g/h), ignoring the Trio; OFF nothing.
- **Power**: off, its valve closes (normally closed); the Trio sees `no-power`.
- **Faults**, each shutting its valve and opening its error relay with the fault named (the Trio shows OFFLINE, fault, with the words): CO₂ GAS ALARM (the room's detector's interlock), NO SUPPLY PRESSURE (inlet below 2 bar, set where the valve still passes most of its flow, so a falling supply is called as what it is), NO WATER FLOW (the injection's booster not running), SIGNAL LOST (a broken loop, in REMOTE only); and two it latches until RESET at the panel: FLOW NOT PROVED (asked to dose and the meter showing under half of it for 30 s of open time) and VALVE STUCK (the meter showing flow it was not told to give for 30 s).
- **Injected faults**: its valve stuck shut (FLOW NOT PROVED follows) or stuck open (it doses its most whatever it is told, VALVE STUCK is called, and only shutting the wall port or the master valve stops it).
- **Output**: grams of CO₂ each step, for the pool's `Additions.co2`, and the same grams drawn from its wall port.

### Its interface

- **The LCD**: a backlit yellow-green STN 2 × 16 character display (the game's `lcd` kind; its backlight out, a dark panel, with the panel unpowered: `setLcdBacklight`), redrawn only when its sixteen-character text changes. Line 1 always shows the measured flow in g/h and a four-letter status word: AUTO, HAND, OFF, or, while its valve is overridden, OPEN or SHUT (Alicat's EXH and HLD in words). Line 2 shows the page MODE has chosen: the set point (`SP AUTO  500 g/h`), the valve drive (`VALVE DRIVE  60%`, about 60 % at full scale on working pressure, 100 % forced open), the inlet pressure (`INLET  3.90 bar`), the totaliser (`TOTAL   1.234 kg`, grams dosed ever, kept in the world), the alarm (`ALM NONE`, or the fault in twelve letters) and the control state (`AUTO OVR NORMAL`).
- **MODE** (a blue membrane key): steps line 2 round its six pages. The page is the display's own, not the world's.
- **▼ ▲** (grey membrane keys): HAND's local set point, 50 g/h a press, 0 to 2,000.
- **RUN** (green, lit while it doses) and **ALARM** (red, lit while its panel names a fault).
- **LOCAL · REMOTE**, first HAND · AUTO (a small rotary selector, the parts library's pointer knob, two detents 45° either side of upright, LOCAL to the left; a toggle until the owner's ruling of 2026-11-01, as the rotary use rule did not suit a toggle; renamed LOCAL · REMOTE the same day, the instrument trade's words for where a controller takes its set point, HAND · OFF · AUTO being a motor starter's; the model keeps its field `hand`, meaning LOCAL, so the save is unchanged; the LCD shows LOCAL or REMOTE on line 1, `SP LOCAL` or `SP REM` on its set point page): AUTO is the panel's REMOTE, following the Trio's 4–20 mA and release; HAND is its MANUAL, at the local set point.
- **The valve OVERRIDE** (a key-operated rotary selector, the parts library's key selector at a third of its size, as forcing switches are, so it cannot be knocked or worked casually; CLOSE 45° anticlockwise, NORM upright, OPEN 45° clockwise, its key's bow the pointer, the key kept in its barrel while the game has no keys to carry; a use on its left half turns it anticlockwise, on its right half clockwise, or LB and RB, with the turn cue; until 2026-11-01 a three-position bat toggle between guard rails): CLOSE holds the valve shut whatever is asked; OPEN drives it fully open whatever its set point (it passes 125 % of its range at working pressure: `CO2_MFC.purge`, its valve sized with headroom over its range, its meter reading to 128 % as Alicat's does).

### Decisions

- **HAND · AUTO and OFF**: the controller's toggle chooses REMOTE (AUTO) or MANUAL (HAND), so the Olympic's control box no longer has the three-way selector: it has an OFF · ON switch instead, the panel's dosing enabled or not, as packaged gas dosing panels have. The doser keeps one `mode` (REMOTE, MANUAL or OFF, as the Trio's `ChannelFeedback` reports it) and a new `hand`, where the toggle stands, kept while the panel is OFF, so switching ON brings back the mode the toggle shows. The Lounge's three-way selector is unchanged, and sets `hand` to follow it.
- **The override in the model**: `override`, CLOSE · NORMAL · OPEN, a world action (`co2/panel` with `{ kind: 'override' }`), saved in the world (`WORLD_VERSION` 69; the migration from 68 sets NORMAL, `hand` from the mode, and the totaliser at nought). Only the mass-flow kind has one. It acts on the valve in place of the set point, as Brooks's VOR and Alicat's hold and exhaust do, so it bypasses the Trio's 4–20 mA and release, HAND's set point, and SIGNAL LOST (which is only about the set point); the panel's proving (FLOW NOT PROVED, VALVE STUCK) is suspended while it is forced, as the controller knows its valve is.
- **Safety**: normal practice is that a forcing input acts below the controller but never above the safety chain. So a forced OPEN is still stopped by the panel being unpowered or switched OFF, the room's gas detector's A1 interlock (CO₂ GAS ALARM), no supply pressure, no water flowing at the injection point and a latched fault; the master valve's trip (the detector's A2) and the wall port, upstream, leave it nothing to pass. Tests: `co2-dosing.test.ts`, "the mass flow controller's own controls".
- **Its alarm**: an active override is shown on the LCD's line 1 (OPEN or SHUT) and its control page, and on the Olympic's annunciator in its old spare way, CO₂ MFC VALVE OVERRIDDEN (`co2-annunciator.ts`, `CO2_OVERRIDE_WINDOW`), and so on the old control room's twin and the bridge's lists. It is a status window, not an alarm (`INFORMATIONAL_INDICATIONS` in `health.ts`): forcing the valve is the operator's own deliberate act at the panel, with the interlocks still in force, so it is shown and logged, ON and OFF (`alarm-events.ts`), as a forced point is on a control system, and the health check does not count it. The action itself is logged too (`describe-action.ts`: "valve OVERRIDE to OPEN: its valve forced fully open"). If the owner would rather it sounded, it is one line to take it out of `INFORMATIONAL_INDICATIONS`.

## Gas detection (`co2-detection.ts`)

CO₂ is an asphyxiant and heavier than air, so each room it is piped into (the loading bay and each dosing plant room) has a fixed infrared (NDIR) CO₂ detector, after Crowcon's Xgard IQ or Dräger's Polytron 2 IR, 0–5 % volume, T90 30 s, its head 0.3 m off the floor. The model follows the toxic gas detection's pattern (`general-safety/logic/gas-detection.ts`): one well-mixed room whose air changes carry the gas off toward where the leak alone would hold it, a detector lagging the air, alarms latched until reset with the air clear, ACCEPT silencing the sounder. It differs where CO₂ does: the air already holds some (420 ppm outdoors, 500 in a plant room), and its alarms are EH40's workplace exposure limits for CO₂ as well as the instantaneous levels, the averages kept as such detectors keep them, in buckets (fifteen one-minute means, thirty-two quarter-hour means).

- **A1 (low)**: the reading at 5,000 ppm, or its 8-hour average at 5,000. The beacon flashes, and the room's dosing valve shuts (`dosingInterlock`).
- **A2 (high)**: the reading at 15,000 ppm, or its 15-minute average at 15,000. The sounder sounds, the extract purges, and the master valve trips (`masterInterlock`), stopping the gas to the whole building.
- **Rooms** (estimates): the loading bay 320 m³ at 3 air changes; the Lounge plant room 600 m³ and the Olympic's 2,250 m³, at 4, purging at 10, 0.3 with the extract stopped. With the extract running a joint leaking 2 kg/h only lifts the Lounge plant room by some 450 ppm; with it stopped, the same leak reaches A1 in a few hours, and a broken joint at the regulator's 10 kg/h reaches A2 in about three.

## Tests

- `co2-supply.test.ts` (toolkit): the manifold's alarms.
- `co2-distribution.test.ts`: the line's starting pressure and gas; ports given what they draw from the bank in use; pressure held with the master shut and nothing drawn; falling within two minutes when the master shuts and dosing goes on, and refilling; the same through the emergency isolation valve, an unpowered master or a tripped one; the trip held until reset; a shut port; cylinders emptying, changing over and calling BANK EMPTY, the line unaffected; both banks spent, SUPPLY EXHAUSTED; port and manifold leaks.
- `co2-dosing.test.ts`: the 4–20 mA and release; the sizes against the Trio's channel; each kind in REMOTE, MANUAL, OFF and unpowered; the solenoid's time-proportional average on fine and coarse steps; every fault, latched and not; the feedback as `ChannelFeedback` and the Trio's `channelStatus` for every OFFLINE reason; and the panel on `stepController`'s `ports.co2`, dosing what it asks in g/h, and shown OFFLINE, still asked, when switched off.

## The experiment: CO₂ first against acid alone (mission 118, step 5)

The owner, 2026-10-31: "we want to see if that improves the situation now that the system has a milder acid to work with." `src/game/co2-experiment.slow.test.ts` runs the new game for 48 hours twice, on the world's 10 s step, after the CO₂ loss correction (`2b2efaa7`) and the Trio's pH anti-windup (`003a770f`): once as built (each Trio's pH demand shared CO₂ first, up to its panel's most, acid trimming the rest) and once with both CO₂ panels' control cables unplugged (acid alone, the plant before mission 118). pH is sampled every 10 minutes against the Trio's set point of 7.3 and its band of 7.2 to 7.4. The chloride is what the acid's HCl adds, over the pool's volume. Set `CO2_EXPERIMENT_OUT` to a file to have the run write its numbers.

| ------------------- | ------- | ----------------- | ------------------ | ------------ | --------- | -------- | ---------- | ------------------ | ---------------- |
| Lounge, acid alone  | 7.284   | 0.032             | 0.088              | 100 %        | 31.1 l    | 0        | +60.9 mg/l | +22.8 mg/l         | −27.5 mg/l       |
| Lounge, CO₂ first   | 7.288   | 0.021             | 0.079              | 100 %        | 0         | 5.5 kg   | +39.9 mg/l | 0                  | +2.7 mg/l        |
| Olympic, acid alone | 7.273   | 0.034             | 0.058              | 100 %        | 242 l     | 0        | +20.8 mg/l | +9.9 mg/l          | −13.1 mg/l       |
| Olympic, CO₂ first  | 7.281   | 0.030             | 0.071              | 100 %        | 0         | 37.4 kg  | +11.3 mg/l | 0                  | +0.2 mg/l        |

**Verdict: yes, the milder acid helps, and most where the acid did harm.** Both ways hold the pH in its band all the time, so the control itself was not the problem once the CO₂ loss and the pH integral were put right; CO₂ holds it a little closer to its set point (the Lounge's mean deviation a third smaller). The gain is in the water's balance: with acid alone the Lounge loses 27.5 mg/l of alkalinity in two days (about 14 a day, so it would leave its 80 to 200 band in about three days from 120 without bicarbonate) and gains 23 mg/l of chloride; with CO₂ its alkalinity holds (CO₂ adds carbonate and takes none away) and its TDS rises a third less. The Olympic shows the same at a fifth of the rate. In this run the CO₂ panels carried the whole pH demand and the acid pumps never ran: the Lounge's 300 g/h rotameter averaged 115 g/h and the Olympic's 2 kg/h controller 780 g/h, so acid trims only peaks larger than a quiet two days brings. The two rooms together draw about 21 kg of CO₂ a day, so a full pair of racks (272 kg) lasts some 12 to 13 days, a swap about every six. The result does not point at the CO₂ loss formula as a remaining cause: with the formula as corrected, acid alone also holds the pH in band; what acid alone cannot do is keep the alkalinity, which is the chemistry of HCl, not of the loss.

### The advice verification with CO₂ connected

The advice verification suite (`doc/plans/aqualis-advice-verification.md`) stays the acid-only baseline. Its CO₂ variant, `aqualis-verification-co2.{lounge,olympic}-{open,closed}.slow.test.ts`, runs the same scenarios with each pool's CO₂ panel on its Trio (`scenarioWorld`'s `co2`). Of the baseline's known failures most remain (the chlorine, thiosulphate, shock, alkalinity-high, dilution and scaling findings, which are not about pH dosing). The differences:

| Suite          | Passes with CO₂ (a baseline known failure)                                                          | Fails with CO₂ only                                                |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Lounge OPEN    | `accuracy:alkalinity-low` (no acid to take the bicarbonate's alkalinity back)                       | `twin:acid-off`                                                    |
| Lounge CLOSED  | `accuracy:alkalinity-low`, `accuracy:aggressive`                                                    | `twin:acid-off`, `loop:aggressive`, `loop:ph-low+alkalinity-low`   |
| Olympic OPEN   | `loop:ph-high+alkalinity-low`, `order:settles` (the undersized acid pump no longer holds the pH up) | `twin:acid-off`, `twin:acid-manual`                                |
| Olympic CLOSED | `loop:aggressive`, `loop:hardness-high`, `loop:ph-low+alkalinity-low`                               | `twin:acid-off`, `twin:acid-manual`, `loop:ph-high+alkalinity-low` |

What the new failures mean (`CO2_WHY` in `aqualis-verification-known.test-support.ts`): with CO₂ on the Trio the acid-pump fault scenarios (`acid-off`, `acid-manual`) stop being faults that matter, since CO₂ carries the pH whether the pump is put right or not, so following the advice is no nearer neutral than leaving it; Dr Aqualis could say the acid pump can wait while CO₂ holds the pH. In the closed Lounge, a bicarbonate dose lifts the pH past its band and CO₂, which takes no alkalinity, brings it back below once before the integral unwinds. In the closed Olympic, with CO₂ carrying the pH the bicarbonate round comes sooner and the chlorine round after it overshoots as the undersized hypochlorite pump catches up. The gains are where the baseline's failures were the acid's: the bicarbonate's alkalinity is no longer taken back by acid, and the Olympic's undersized acid pump no longer limits the pH.
