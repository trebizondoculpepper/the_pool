# Does Dr Aqualis's advice work?

The owner's mission (2026-10-31): tests that tell us whether the Trio's water advisory model (`src/game/plant/aqualis-advisor.ts`) actually works, and fixes to whatever they show is wrong. The advisor makes three claims, diagnosis (what is out of band, and how badly), prescription (Show dose: what to add, how much, and where) and ranking (what to do first); this plan tests each, and the closed loop of an operator following it. The owner ordered the independent reference cases first (2026-10-31): they tether the most basic principles to reality before anything is built on them.

**Findings.** Nothing disagrees materially, so neither the chemistry nor the advisor was changed at this stage:

- The model's stoichiometry (bicarbonate, soda ash, calcium chloride, hypochlorite, dilution) is exact to the hand arithmetic; its carbonate equilibria agree with the textbook's within 1 % in the acid demand and 0.01 pH in the rises; its LSI is Carrier's formula exactly, and within the factor tables' 0.1 steps.
- Soda ash's Show dose runs 4 % over the hand figure, from the model's pK₁ of 6.36 at 25 °C against the hand 6.35: within tolerance.
- At pool conditions (29 °C, TDS 1000) the same 101.8 g of HCl moves the pH 0.092, not 0.100: the activity coefficients and temperature, which the hand arithmetic leaves out. This is the model being more exact, not a disagreement.
- Thiosulphate: the model is fitted to PWTAG TN23's 5 g/m³ per mg/l (`CHEMISTRY.thiosulphateToSulphate`, 0.057). That is far above the stoichiometry to sulphate alone (0.875 g/m³) and above US trade figures (1.2 to 2 g/m³): PWTAG's figure allows for tetrathionate and a margin. UK practice is followed; recorded, not changed.
- Calcium chloride is dosed as anhydrous (100 %) in the model and in Show dose; the commoner flake (77 to 80 %) would want about 1.3 times the weight. Show dose names "calcium chloride" without a strength. Recorded.
- Show dose rounds to three significant figures (10.07 shows as 10.1 kg).

## Part 1: hand doses fed at a rate

**The problem.** A `hand/dose` into the balance tank dissolved at once. The owner measured it (2026-10-31): 17 kg of bicarbonate into the Lounge's tank, about 9 m³ of the circuit's 146, took the tank to 1,150 mg/l alkalinity and pH 8.07 within a minute; the Trio, whose sample tapping is just downstream, read 820 mg/l, pH 7.82 and TDS 2,057, and raised pH HIGH and WATER SCALING for 10 to 20 minutes.

**What changed.** A first version dissolved every solid automatically over half an hour a sack; the owner replaced it the same day: a dose goes in at once, as before, unless it is given a rate. `dose pool_01 bicarbonate 17kg 1kg/min` (the world action's `rate`, the chemical's unit a minute: kg/min, g/min, l/min, ml/min, written with or without a space, before or after the place) feeds it in: the world holds the feed (`DoseCharge` in `treatment.charges`, by pool; `WORLD_VERSION` 65) and puts `rate × dt` of it into the water at each chemistry tick until it is all in (`releaseCharges`, `water-treatment.ts`). The action's line logs its start ("17 kg sodium bicarbonate being fed into the balance tank at 1kg/min (17 min)") and a watcher its finish ("17 kg sodium bicarbonate: feed finished, all in"); the console's `doses` lists the feeds under way, how far each is and how long it has left. A second feed of the same chemical into the same place **queues** behind the first: normal practice is one batch after another through the same feed point, and a second instruction is more of the chemical, not a change of mind (a mistaken feed is stopped by hand, which can come with the inventory game). Feeds of different chemicals, or into different places, go in together.

A dose into the pool, at once or fed, is spread over its surface, all three cells by their shares (it went only into the return side before); CO₂ by hand is still gassed in at the injection point. A save from version 64, whose doses dissolved automatically, has what was still dissolving put in at once.

Dilution is a new stand-in world action, `hand/dilute` (the console's `dilute <pool> <m³>`), the CLAUDE.md rule for undesigned features: there is a `pool/drain-down` action, but it stops the circulation and drains the pool to the sewer, so no world action could carry out "replace this much water". It is a feed too: the circuit's water drained to waste and replaced with the mains' at the pool's make-up rate (`poolMakeUpRate`: the Lounge's 9 m³/h, the Olympic's 30), every zone by its volume. It does not move the water levels, the meters or the sewer's record; a designed drain-and-refill would.

**Show dose says how, and at what rate** (`DoseAmount.how`, `visits`, `first`, `rate`): "Feed it into the balance tank at 120g/min (about 2 h)", "Feed it into the balance tank at 40ml/min (about 1 h), acid into water", "Spread it across the pool at …", "Drain it down to waste and refill with fresh water (about 6 h at the make-up's rate)", and for the pumps' own chemicals while they are online, "The acid pump is doing this; leave it to work." The rate is written as `dose` takes it, so it can be typed straight in. It is the visit's amount over one turnover (`feedRate`), rounded down to two figures: then the water the Trio samples from the tank rises no further than the whole circuit will once mixed, and the pumps keep pace with what it does to the pH. Only a shock (superchlorination) goes in faster, over a quarter of a turnover, at least 10 minutes. (At first a dose spread over a closed pool went in at the shock's pace too; Part 4 found its bicarbonate's pH lift outpacing the acid pump.)

**Per visit** (`PER_VISIT`, `aqualis-advisor.ts`). Normal practice (PWTAG's Code of Practice and the trade's guidance) is to correct the balance by hand in stages, into the balance tank, a turnover and a retest between, so that no one addition clouds the water, overshoots, or swings the pH under bathers. It gives no single figure, and no citable one was found, so these are this plan's, chosen so that a water just out of band is put right in one visit and anything further is split: with the pool open, alkalinity moved at most 50 mg/l at a visit, calcium hardness 150 mg/l, the pH 0.2 by hand, and fresh water at most a working day's make-up (8 hours of it); closed, twice as much (Part 2). A dose over the limit says so and gives the first visit's amount: "Too much at once: 12.6 kg now, retest after a turnover (2 h) and repeat, 2 visits." The Olympic's bicarbonate from 50 to 120 mg/l, over 200 kg, is two visits.

**The measurement again** (`hand-dose.slow.test.ts`: the Lounge on its settled water, 17 kg of bicarbonate, the Trio watched for four hours):

**The pH HIGH that was left: a fix to the Trio's controller.** Fed slowly, the spike at the sample was gone, but the pH still crept just over the Trio's 7.6 alarm. That was not the feed but the bicarbonate itself (closed-system arithmetic: alkalinity 51 → 120 at pH 7.3 takes the pH to about 7.65 unless acid holds it) and the Trio's pH control, proportional only, its full scale (`ACID_G_PER_M3`, 1.5 g of HCl per m³ an hour) about 28 % of the Lounge's acid pump: it could not carry the acid demand bicarbonate (and the model's fast CO₂ loss) put on it, the same cause as `new-game-normal.md`'s first finding. It was tractable, so the Trio's pH is now PI (`aqualis-controller.ts`): an integral, `PH_INTEGRAL_HOURS` (1 h) at the reference turnover and stretched with it as chlorine's is, up to `PH_INTEGRAL_REACH` (4) bands, never below nought, held while the acid is pinned at its pump's most, offline or inhibited, and inside the dead band. It learns the steady acid demand, so the pH comes back to its set point rather than sitting above it. The Trios' three-day test with bathers now holds the Olympic's mean pH within 0.05 of 7.3, nearer than the old controller's 7.358 (`aqualis-world.slow.test.ts`). The integral is world state (the Trio's controller's `phIntegral`, nought in a migrated save).

## Part 2: the pool OPEN or CLOSED

The Trio's settings (Settings › Pool dynamics) have a third button, **Pool: OPEN** in green or **Pool: CLOSED** in red; a press turns it over. It is world state (`TrioState.poolOpen`, `WORLD_VERSION` 65, open in a new game and a migrated save), the action `aqualis` `{ act: 'pool', open }`, logged as "pool OPEN" or "pool CLOSED"; dev58's rig has its own.

**Unified with First Aid.** First Aid's programmes already said whether they need the pool closed (`FIRST_AID[id].closePool`: formed stool, diarrhoea, breakpoint shock and bring chlorine down do; the dosing pause does not), but only in words. Now starting one that does closes the pool, and the pool cannot be opened while one runs; when it ends the pool stays closed until the operator opens it, as in practice the pool reopens only after the water is checked.

**While the pool is open**, bather safety bounds every instruction: hand doses go into the balance tank, never the pool (PWTAG: no chemicals into the pool with bathers in it), at the per-visit limits of Part 1; a chlorine far above its band is an alarm ("nobody should swim in that!"); superchlorination, which takes the free chlorine to ten times the combined, is an explicit override that needs the pool closed, so its advice is "Close the pool and superchlorinate it…", its Show dose "Close the pool first. Spread it across the pool…".

**While the pool is closed** (`AdviseOptions.open`):

- Doses may be larger: the per-visit limits doubled (alkalinity 100 mg/l, hardness 300 mg/l, pH 0.4 by hand), so a correction that wants two visits open takes one closed.
- Doses go straight into the pool, spread over its surface, where the Trio's tapping sees them only mixed (at the same rate, over a turnover: Part 4).
- A free chlorine far above its band is no alarm with nobody in the water, only a warning that the pool stays closed until it is back under its high limit; thiosulphate is offered, not demanded.
- Dr Aqualis says what must be true before it reopens (`reopenUnmet`; advice `pool-closed`, information): normal practice after PWTAG's guidance on reopening after an incident or a shock, judged against the normal targets even while First Aid's are worked to: free chlorine back in its band (1.0–1.5 mg/l by default), combined chlorine no more than its limit (0.5), pH in its band (7.2–7.4), and no First Aid programme still running that needs the pool closed. For example: "The pool is closed. Before it reopens: free chlorine 1.00–1.50 (now 6.00); pH 7.20–7.40 (now 7.60)." With all met: "The pool is closed, and the water is fit to swim in. It may reopen (Settings › Pool dynamics)."

The cabinet's alarms (`alarms`) are the same open or closed: they are the analyser's limits, and a real controller's alarm relay does not know whether the pool is open.

## Part 3: ranking, safety first

The owner's ruling (2026-10-31), as `order` in `aqualis-advisor.ts` now does it, each advice carrying its `tier`:

- **Tier 1, health and safety**: free chlorine below its band (too low to disinfect); free chlorine far above it (beyond `ALARM_LIMITS`, twice its high) while the pool is open; a pH far out (beyond `ALARM_LIMITS`, 0.2 past its band) while the pool is open; the hypochlorite offline; the acid or CO₂ offline while the pH is already high (its chlorine losing its bite).
- **Tier 2, water balance and comfort**: everything else out of band, chlorine a little high, combined chlorine, alkalinity, hardness, the LSI, TDS and sulphate, and the acid or CO₂ offline with the pH still in band.
- **Tier 3, information**: no CO₂ connected, every reading in its band, the pool closed and what it needs to reopen.
- **Within a tier, the biggest difference first**: each reading's distance from its set point in the half-band on its side (`deviation`: 1 at the band's edge), the same metric Part 4 scores the water by. It needs no dose arithmetic, so the ranking stays live (Part 5).
- **Overrides.** A closed pool relaxes the bather-safety tier: high chlorine and a far pH are tier 2 there; chlorine too low stays tier 1, as the water must be fit before it reopens. In superchlorination (any First Aid programme but the dosing pause: `superchlorinating`), high chlorine is intended, so it is never tier 1.
- **Order that normal practice imposes**, which overrides the biggest-first rule: a cause before what it causes (an offline pump before the reading it lets drift; alkalinity and hardness before the balance they make), and **alkalinity before pH** (PWTAG and every trade guide: the alkalinity is the pH's buffer; correct it, then the pH). A cause takes the tier and, a hair over, the size of what it comes before when those are greater, so alkalinity low comes before a pH high that is further out, and is lifted to tier 1 with a pH far out. That is where normal practice and biggest-first conflict, and normal practice wins.

**The bicarbonate with the pH high** (the owner, in play; revised by Part 4, below): "add sodium bicarbonate" while the pH reads high looks to a player as if it will push the pH higher, and it does, a little (towards about 8.3). So when the bicarbonate is advised with the pH at or above its set point, Dr Aqualis says so: "Raise it with sodium bicarbonate. This will lift the pH a little; the acid pump will bring it back down." (the CO₂, where that is online). With the acid offline, normal practice is still the alkalinity first, then the pH by hand: "Raise it with sodium bicarbonate, then lower the pH with acid by hand: the bicarbonate lifts it a little.", and the pH advice, by hand into the balance tank, follows it. Below its set point, the plain "Raise the alkalinity with sodium bicarbonate." Part 4 found that with the pH already above its band the bicarbonate's lift takes it past its alarm limit (7.75 on both pools): there the pH comes first, the acid pump's ("Let the acid pump bring the pH into its band first; then raise the alkalinity with sodium bicarbonate.") or acid by hand with the pump offline ("Lower the pH with acid by hand first; then raise the alkalinity with sodium bicarbonate."), ranked before it; and a visit's bicarbonate is held to what lifts the pH, unbuffered, no higher than 0.1 inside its alarm limit. The one place bather safety overrides alkalinity before pH.

`aqualis-ranking.test.ts` checks each rule.

## Part 5: the solver run only on request

**Before.** Show dose is a bisection over `dose()` (`gramsFor`, 60 iterations) for most additives, or `acidFor` (the same over `solvePh`). `advise` worked out every advice's dose on every call. Measured (Node, this machine, mean of 500 calls): 0.002 ms with every reading in its band, 0.044 ms with one problem (alkalinity low), 0.17 ms with five (pH, alkalinity, hardness, TDS and free chlorine out). The plant room's cabinet (`aqualis-room-cabinet.ts`) works out its display's inputs, the advice among them, once for each new Trio state, cached on the state's identity; but the Trio's state is a new object every world step (its computer's clock), and the cabinet is driven every frame (`driveCabinet` asks whether the doctor is shown), so it ran once a frame, about 60 times a second, for each cabinet in the room: up to 10 ms a second for a pool well out of balance. dev58 ran it on each tick of its rig (every 10 s of the rig's time) and after each press.

**The screen today.** Show dose is a button on the doctor's page; pressing it shows the amount for the advice on show (`showDose` in the display state), and turning to another advice or another page hides it. Its button is greyed where the advice has no dose.

**After.** `advise(…, { doses: false })` gives the cheap list: which readings are out of band, their severity, tier and order (the ranking needs no dose arithmetic: its size is each reading's deviation, Part 3), the advice's words and where it goes, and `hasDose` for the button, but no amount. The amounts are closures (`prescribe`), worked out only when asked: `doseFor(id, …)` gives one advice's, the same as `advise` with doses gives it (`aqualis-lazy-dose.test.ts` checks every advice of five waters, open and closed). The cabinet and dev58 ask for live advice without doses, and for a dose only while it is shown: the doctor's page, Show dose pressed, on that advice (`withShownDose`). The amount is cached against the readings, settings, channels and options it was worked out for (`createDoseCache`; dev58 by identity), so it is worked out once for each new reading, every 10 s of world time while it is on screen, and not at all otherwise. The alarms (`alarms`) never used the solver and are unchanged. `advise` still works the doses out by default, for the tests and anything else that wants them all at once.

**The score** (`score`, `aqualis-advice.test-support.ts`): the sum, over every reading with a target (free and combined chlorine, pH, alkalinity, hardness, the LSI, TDS, sulphate), of its distance from its set point in the half-band on its side, so 0 at the set point and 1 at its band's edge; combined chlorine, TDS and sulphate, whose set points are a floor or a working value, count only above them. Follow versus don't scores the pool's far side (what bathers meet, `farSide`); the closed loop scores and judges the Trio's readings, what the operator sees.

**The scenarios** (`SCENARIOS`), each set up in every zone of a new game's water, the analyser left ten minutes to settle: free chlorine 0.5 and 4; pH 7.55 and 7.0; alkalinity 50 and 250; hardness 70 and 1100 (only dilution); TDS 1800; combined chlorine 1.2 (superchlorination, the pool closed by the override); the pairs pH 7.0 with alkalinity 40, pH 7.55 with alkalinity 50 (the owner's), aggressive water (hardness 80, alkalinity 60, pH 7.2: LSI about −1), scaling water (hardness 900, alkalinity 190, pH 7.5: about +0.8); the acid pump in its own manual mode or stopped at its keys, the pH high; the hypochlorite pump gas-locked, its flow monitor tripped, the chlorine low.

**A known failure** is a finding the advice cannot put right (the model, the plant or the Trio's controller); each is listed with its reason (`aqualis-verification-known.test-support.ts`) and run as `it.fails`, so it is still seen to fail and a fix that makes it pass must take it off the list.

### What the suite found, and what changed

The first runs found these bad instructions, each fixed in the advisor (or the operator's words) and checked by the suite after:

- **A pH a little low, with the acid pump online, was given soda ash.** In this model the pH rises on its own as the water gives up CO₂ (fast in the Lounge), so the soda ash overshot: not following ended nearer neutral (2.55 against 3.70). Normal practice for a slightly low pH under acid dosing is to check the acid pump and let aeration bring it up: Dr Aqualis now says "It will rise on its own as the water gives up its CO₂; check the acid pump is not overdosing.", and gives soda ash only for a pH far low (past `ALARM_LIMITS`), aimed halfway from the band's foot to its set point, as the water finishes the rise.
- **High alkalinity on the Olympic was left to its undersized acid pump**, and its pH climbed to its alarm; **scaling water** likewise. A pump flat out (`PINNED`, 98 % of its most) against a structural demand (alkalinity at or above its set point, whose CO₂ loss keeps lifting the pH) is now helped by hand: "The acid pump is flat out and cannot catch up: help it by hand." For the hypochlorite, and for acid at a lower alkalinity, a hand dose gained nothing (the pump backs off: accuracy 0.02) and added to the overshoot, so it is not given.
- **High alkalinity on the Lounge by hand** only stood in for the acid its pump would give (accuracy −0.17): it is the acid pump's, unless flat out, as above. By hand, a visit's acid is held to what takes the pH no lower than its band's foot (0.2 below closed).
- **Thiosulphate removed only half its aim** in the Lounge: the Trio, seeing the tank's dechlorinated water, ran its hypochlorite pump and put the chlorine back. Dr Aqualis already said to stop the hypochlorite; now he says how: "Stop the hypochlorite (First Aid › Bring chlorine down) and take it down with sodium thiosulphate.", and the operator does. Its dose now aims at the top of the band (1.5), not the set point, as the chlorine goes on falling on its own and aiming lower undershot.
- **Bicarbonate fed into water already high in pH** took the pH to 7.75, past its 7.6 alarm, on both pools (the owner's own check); on the Olympic, aggressive water's bicarbonate overshot past its alarm too. A visit's bicarbonate is now held to what lifts the pH, unbuffered, no higher than 0.1 inside its alarm limit (0.1 past it closed); and with the pH already above its band, the pH comes first, the pump's (or acid by hand, the pump offline), the bicarbonate after: "Let the acid pump bring the pH into its band first; then raise the alkalinity with sodium bicarbonate." This is the one place normal practice's alkalinity-before-pH gives way: to bather safety.
- **A gas-locked pump** was told "Clear its fault and reset it at the pump", which does not clear gas: now "Gas in its head: open its bleed, acknowledge it, and shut the bleed once it pumps liquid." (and "Its day tank is empty: fill it, then acknowledge the pump." for an empty tank).
- **"Turn the hypochlorite down"** while the pump is under the Trio's control, which the operator cannot do: now "The hypochlorite pump has stopped; it will fall on its own."
- **A spread over a closed pool** at a quarter of a turnover pushed the pH out of the top of its band (bicarbonate's lift outpacing the pump): every feed now goes in over a turnover; only a shock goes faster.
- **The dilution stand-in** (Part 1) worked as a bleed, the water drained mixed with the fresh, so 142 m³ replaced only about 62 % of the water where Dr Aqualis's arithmetic, and his words ("drain it down and refill"), are a batch: it is now a drain-down and refill, the drained water the pool's as it was.

### Findings not fixed here

Every instruction the advisor gave could be carried out by a world action (`hand/dose`, the new `hand/dilute`, the pumps' keys, bleed and acknowledge, a First Aid programme, the pool OPEN or CLOSED); none is a finding of that kind. The known failures, by cause:

- **The Lounge's chlorine overshoots once its hypochlorite pump is dosing again** (0.5 → about 2.1 mg/l; and after a gas-locked pump is put right): the Trio's chlorine correction (`CHLORINE_CORRECTION_HOURS`, a quarter of an hour) against the Lounge's dead time, a turnover (1.6 h) between the dose at the return and the sample at the tank, with a pump that can lift the pool 2 mg/l an hour. Tried: the correction no quicker than the turnover took the overshoot down to 1.68, but with bathers the Lounge's chlorine then fell to 0.72 and eight controller, loop and world tests failed; half the turnover, 1.76. Reverted: the trade between overshoot and holding a busy session is a tuning decision for the owner. The advice is right (let the pump work); the loop's overshoot is the controller's.
- **The model's CO₂ loss** (`co2LossConstant`, Wojtowicz's fit far past its turnovers; `new-game-normal.md`'s first finding) is behind most of the rest. The acid pump takes back a third or more of the bicarbonate's alkalinity in the Lounge as it holds the pH (its prescription accuracy 0.60 to 0.65; the Olympic's 0.85 to 0.95); soda ash's lift is taken back as the pH rises on its own (the Olympic's accuracy about 0.01); the alkalinity drifts down about 1 mg/l an hour, so a restarted acid pump or a thiosulphate dose can tip it out of its band; bicarbonate with the CO₂ loss takes a pH from 7.0 past the top of its band (the pH-low-and-alkalinity-low loops). Not fixed: it is the chemistry model's, Hall 4's quiz is built on it, and its fix wants the bench re-run (`TODO.md`).
- **The Olympic's undersized pumps** (the owner's choice, mission 101): its acid pump, even helped by hand, cannot bring alkalinity 250 into its band in ten rounds, nor hold the pH of scaling water (alkalinity 190) against the CO₂ loss, nor take a pH of 7.55 down fast enough for the bicarbonate to follow in ten rounds with the pool open; its hypochlorite pump cannot at once make up the chlorine a dilution's fresh water thins, nor catch the chlorine after a shock's thiosulphate.
- **A superchlorination's caustic soda** lifts the pH out of its band (with the pool closed for it, as it should be); **thiosulphate's** chlorine goes on falling on its own, so its accuracy against not following is off its aim and in the Lounge open the chlorine falls below its band before the pump catches it.
- **The Trio's pH integral**, wound up bringing a pH of 7.55 down, takes it below its band before it unwinds (the Lounge open, pH high with alkalinity low; and the Lounge's scaling water, open and closed, from 7.5). No anti-windup acts, as the pump is not flat out and no hand dose is being fed: it is the integral time against the turnover, a tuning decision ("The Trio's pH anti-windup", below).
- **Ranking with three problems** (alkalinity 60, hardness 80, TDS 1700): within the balance tier the ranking goes by each reading's own distance from its set point, cheap and live (Part 5); the bicarbonate comes first (distance 1.5) and improves the score least (0.3 to 1.0), the dilution for TDS most (up to 3.1, the mains water lifting the hardness and alkalinity too). Ranking by predicted improvement would need the dose arithmetic, so the ranking too would be worked out on request; not done here, for the owner.
- **Dilution's time.** Show dose's "about 8 h at the make-up's rate" takes the make-up as a tenth of the entered circulation, as the Trio knows nothing else; the Olympic's make-up is 30 m³/h, not 62, so its "a day of make-up" visit of 496 m³ takes about 16 hours. A make-up rate in the Trio's settings would put it right.
- **Two strengths** (stage 1): thiosulphate as PWTAG TN23's 5 g/m³ a mg/l, well above stoichiometry and US trade figures; calcium chloride as anhydrous, where flake (77 to 80 %) would want about 1.3 times the weight.
- **The Trio's sample at the tank sees a dose fed into the tank.** How far depends on the rate, not the amount (Part 1); fed at Dr Aqualis's rate, a visit's dose keeps its alarms off.

## CO₂ loss reference cases (mission 118)

The owner's top priority (2026-11-01), before CO₂ dosing: the model lost CO₂ from the water far too fast, and so the acid pumps worked several times as hard as a real pool's (`new-game-normal.md`'s first two findings, most of Part 4's known failures). His rule: tether it to reality first. `src/game/water/co2-loss-reference.test.ts` holds the cases below; the numbers in it are these, worked by hand from the sources, not by the model. Before the fix 19 of its 23 cases failed (run as `it.fails` in the commit that added them).

**The sources.**

- **Wojtowicz's own paper, read directly**: J. A. Wojtowicz, "Swimming Pool Water Balance – Part 3: Factors Affecting Loss of Carbon Dioxide", _Journal of the Swimming Pool and Spa Industry_ 1(3):19–26, 1995, reprinted as chapter 4.3 of _The Chemistry and Treatment of Swimming Pool and Spa Water_ (2001), whose date the model had taken ([poolhelp.com](https://poolhelp.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/4-3-Factors-Affecting-Loss-of-CO2.pdf)). His data: one **outdoor**, bather-free residential pool of 6,800 gal (25.7 m³), sand filter, 80–84 °F, cyanuric acid 69 ppm, hardness 440, carbonate alkalinity 54–96, pump about 35 gpm, runs of 7 days with no acid. His surface/volume ratio was 0.28 ft⁻¹ (the reciprocal of a 3.57 ft mean depth), which is **0.919 m⁻¹**; the model had 0.67 m⁻¹, so every constant it gave was 1.37 times his. First-order constants drifted with time and alkalinity, second-order ones did not, so he fitted `dC/dt = −k·(excess)²`. His turnover dependence, `k = 0.0341 + 0.009·TR + 0.0016·TR²` (ppm·day)⁻¹, is a quadratic through **three** pump duty cycles only: off, 8 h and 24 h a day, TR 0, 2.47 and 7.41 (Table 3, observed 0.034, 0.066, 0.19). His general equation multiplies it by (SVR/0.28 ft⁻¹) and 4296.4·e^(−2516.36/T), an Arrhenius term at 5.0 kcal/mol (Bohr 1899) that is 1 at 27.65 °C. His Table 2: at 24 h a day, carbonate alkalinity 54, 79, 96, the pH rose 7.33 → 7.82, 7.39 → 8.03, 7.33 → 8.03 in a week; a cover cut k to 0.016; the heater did nothing measurable. His Table 5: CO₂ loss was 92 to 96 % of the acid used in 189 residential and test pools in Florida and California. Wind was not measured; bathers were left out.
- **Hot tubs.** Wojtowicz ("Swimming Pool and Spa Water Chemical Adjustments", JSPSI 5(1):39–56, 2004): in spas "the upward drift is accelerated by the higher temperature and use of aeration", with no rate. TroubleFreePool's community (second-hand, labelled as such): a hot tub at alkalinity 80–120, no borates, runs from pH 7.6 to over 8 within 2 to 4 hours of its jets running.
- **Calm and stirred water**: gas transfer velocities for CO₂ at calm surfaces, k600 0.55 to 0.65 cm/h in calm conditions and 1.6 to 2.8 cm/h in light summer winds (a subtropical pond, _Tellus B_ 66:23795, 2014, seen in its abstract); Cole and Caraco's 2.07 cm/h at no wind (_Limnology and Oceanography_ 43:647, 1998). Wojtowicz's own pool, back-calculated (apparent K_L = k × excess × depth): about 0.9 cm/h with the pump off, about 5 cm/h at 24 h a day at an excess of 5 to 7 mg/l, and so about 8 to 9 cm/h at the excess of 9 mg/l that a pool at pH 7.3 and alkalinity 120 carries.
- **PWTAG**: alkalinity 80–200 (Code of Practice); TN6 (_Carbon dioxide for pH control_, 2011) and TN40 (_Choosing an acid pH correctant_, 2018) give no quantities, TN6 only a cost ("a 25m pool is unlikely to cost much more than £2,000 a year for pH reduction"). **Not found**: a citable figure for a commercial indoor pool's daily acid or CO₂ use (the Air Products consumption tables were not readable; Gomà et al., _Chemosphere_ 2010, was paywalled), a measured K_L at an indoor pool surface, a measured K_La for spa jets or blowers, or a weekly alkalinity loss for indoor pools. So the acid figures below are derived, from Wojtowicz's law within its range, not checked against a trade number; that is the gap left.

**Where the sources disagree**, and what was chosen: his pool was outdoors (wind) and bather-free; ours are indoors (no wind) with bathers. No source separates the two; normal practice treats a busy indoor surface as no calmer than a residential outdoor one, so his constants are taken as they stand for both, without a bather term. Turnover beyond his 7.41 a day has no data at all: the returns of a pool already turned over three times as often as a residential pool stir no more of its surface (the agitation is the inlets' at its surface, not the water's passes through the plant), so the constant is held at its 7.41 value. A spa's jets and air are an aeration of their own, fitted to the hot-tub figure.
