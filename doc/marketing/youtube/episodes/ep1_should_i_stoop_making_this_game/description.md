# Should I Keep Making This Game? — The Pool, Episode 1

I'm making a very strange game about swimming pools. Is it too weird for the world?

Play the latest version: https://trebizondoculpepper.github.io/the_pool/

The Pool began with a question I couldn't shake while playing Poolrooms games: if one of those beautiful, impossible, tiled spaces were real, what machinery would it take to keep the water clean, warm and safe? A swimming pool is a machine you can climb inside without noticing. Behind the tiles are pumps, filters, tanks, valves, chemical dosing systems, instruments and control panels. I wanted to make a game about all of that hidden technology.

In this first episode, I'll show you what the last few weeks of work have produced. We visit a small Jacuzzi and its controls, an Olympic-size pool's sand filters and balance tank, and some of the different eras of interface design I've tried to recreate. The rule I'm setting myself is that the machinery should actually do something: switches should switch, gauges should read, and even the music should come from a player in the world. This has turned out to be a much larger undertaking than I expected.

Then we head upstairs to PLANTROOMS, the game's rather peculiar museum of pool engineering. There's an interactive tour of heating and filtration, a pool chemistry challenge that includes a faecal incident, and a skimmer that yields discoveries nobody asked for. It's part earnest simulation, part strange joke, and part celebration of control panels.

I need your honest verdict. Would you play a game about the machinery behind a swimming pool? Which part would you want to try, and which parts lose you? Tell me whether I should keep making it.
